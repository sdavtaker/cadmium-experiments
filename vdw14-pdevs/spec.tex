  \quad
  \text{EOC} = \{(K.\texttt{sum} \to C.\texttt{out})\}
\]

Expected invariant: every output of $K$ equals $10$.

\section*{Cadmium PDEVS Adaptation}

Cadmium implements full Parallel DEVS (PDEVS) with \emph{typed ports}.  Each
port carries a specific message type; couplings are expressed as typed
\texttt{(sender-port, receiver-port)} pairs verified at compile time.
Multiple simultaneous messages are delivered as a \emph{message bag}
(\texttt{std::vector} per port).

\subsection*{Port Types}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
  \toprule
  Model & Port & Type \\
  \midrule
  $G_{1/10}$ & \texttt{out}   & \texttt{out\_port<int>} \\
  $G_{1}$    & \texttt{out}   & \texttt{out\_port<reset\_tick>} \\
  $K$        & \texttt{add}   & \texttt{in\_port<int>} \\
             & \texttt{reset} & \texttt{in\_port<reset\_tick>} \\
             & \texttt{sum}   & \texttt{out\_port<int>} \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Distinct output types on $G_{1/10}$ and $G_1$ enforce the routing contract
at compile time: only integer ticks can reach \texttt{add} and only
\texttt{reset\_tick} tokens can reach \texttt{reset}.

\subsection*{Counter Transitions}

PDEVS delivers all simultaneous messages in a single external (or confluence)
call.  At every integer second $G_{1/10}$ and $G_1$ fire together; the counter
receives both \texttt{add=1} and \texttt{reset=reset\_tick} in the same bag:

\[
  \delta^K_{\text{ext}}(s, e, \langle \texttt{add}:[1],\; \texttt{reset}:[\cdot] \rangle)
  = (n + 1,\; \text{ready})
\]

The \textbf{confluence function} governs the case where $K$ also has a pending
internal event at the same time:
\[
  \delta^K_{\text{con}}(s, e, x) =
    \delta^K_{\text{ext}}\!\left(\delta^K_{\text{int}}(s),\; 0,\; x\right)
\]
Internal transition fires first (clears the ready flag, resets the accumulator),
then external input is applied.  This is the standard ``internal-first''
priority, equivalent to $G_{1/10} \prec G_1$.

\subsection*{Implementation Notes}

The standard \texttt{cadmium::basic\_models::pdevs::accumulator<int,TIME>} is
used directly for $K$.  Concrete generator classes (\texttt{tick\_gen<TIME>},
\texttt{reset\_gen<TIME>}) are defined in the experiment source; their
\texttt{period()} returns \texttt{TIME\{1\}/TIME\{10\}} and \texttt{TIME\{1\}}
respectively, so the same source file exercises both \texttt{float} and rational
time types.

Outputs are captured by driving the \texttt{pdevs::coordinator} directly and
inspecting \texttt{outbox\_port<sum>} after each \texttt{collect\_outputs} call.

\section*{Expected Observations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Float time:} IEEE~754 cannot represent $1/10$ exactly.
        Accumulated error over $\sim$10 additions shifts occasional $G_{1/10}$
        ticks across a 1-second boundary; $K$ outputs $9$ or $11$.
        (Observed: ${\approx}1.4\%$ error rate over 10\,000 resets.)
  \item \textbf{Rational time:} $K$ always outputs $10$; the histogram is a
        single bar at $10$.  Requires a \texttt{TIME} type satisfying the
        \texttt{cadmium::concepts::Time} concept (totally ordered, arithmetic,
        with \texttt{std::numeric\_limits<T>::infinity()}).
\end{itemize}
